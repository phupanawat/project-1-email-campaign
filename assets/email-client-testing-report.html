Email Client Testing Findings

COMP 584 · Project 1 Email Campaign · Northstar Travel "Fall Weekend Escapes"

1. What was tested

The campaign email (index.html) has these features, and each one was checked in every view:

  • Two-column component: "Featured stays" cards.
  • Three-column component: "Trending this fall" destinations.
  • Responsive layout: columns stack under 620px and text sizes shrink.
  • Interactivity: hover states on buttons, nav links, text links, footer links and card images.
  • Keyframe animations: flying plane (translate + rotate), wiggling promo badge (rotate), glowing button (background-color + box-shadow), sliding color stripe (background-position), sliding panel (translateY). No opacity or scale.
  • Checked-box technique: "Find your vibe" tabs built from radio buttons, labels and divs.

5. What worked and what didn't

Worked

  • Table-based two- and three-column layouts held up in every view.
  • Columns stacked and text resized at mobile width.
  • The background image section and outlined buttons rendered the same everywhere.
  • Where the radio technique is supported, the tabs switched panels correctly.

Didn't work

  • Gmail showed no keyframe animations. The plane stayed still and the button had no glow.
  • Gmail removed the radio inputs, so the interactive tabs never appeared.
  • Gmail swapped the plane character for its own emoji image, which changed its color.

6. Takeaways

  • Gmail is the most restrictive client tested. It keeps the layout but drops animation and form-based interactivity.
  • Building the interactive parts as progressive enhancements paid off. Gmail readers still see all three trip ideas, just without tabs.
  • Layout features are safe to rely on. Animation and interactivity should be treated as a bonus for clients that support them.
